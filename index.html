01 — Scroll-Based

Animations driven by scroll position — parallax, sticky, scrub, snap, and storytelling.

Parallax Depth-of-Field
Five mountain layers with parallax translation and blur-based depth-of-field driven by scroll.
Parallax Scrolling
Four SVG layers at different scroll speeds create a 3D depth illusion; adjustable speed multipliers.
Reverse-Scrolling Columns
A center column scrolls normally while two flanking columns scroll in reverse, looping infinitely.
Cover Card to Fixed Header
A hero cover card scrubs into a compact fixed header — height, font size, opacity, blur from one eased value.
Fly-in Fly-out Contact List
Rows translate and fade through entry, active, and exit zones as you scroll — continuous, bidirectional.
Stacking Cards
Cards fan into a deck — per-index sticky offset creates the peek tab, CSS Scroll-Driven Animations drive scale.
ScrollTrigger Animation
The onEnter / onLeave lifecycle demonstrated with vanilla JS — four zones, fade, scrub, pin, and snap.
Scrub Animation
A camera lens disassembles in five stages as you scroll — every transform welded to scroll position, no autoplay.
Pin Animation
A phone mockup pins to the viewport while four features swap in sequence — CSS sticky, no JS pinning.
Snap Scrolling
CSS scroll-snap-type creates a paginated, magnetic feel — switch between mandatory, proximity, and none.
Scrollytelling
Fractional chapter progress drives continuous depth crossfades across six ocean-layer chapters.
Reveal on Scroll
Seven reveal techniques fired by IntersectionObserver at a configurable trigger line.
Stagger Reveal
Groups reveal with cascading delays — forward, reverse, center-out, and random across grid, list, and chips.
Horizontal Scroll
Vertical scroll translates a horizontal panel strip — a tall pinned section provides the scroll budget.
Sticky Section
The whole section pins while its interior morphs through four states scrubbed to scroll position.
Counter Animation
Numbers count 0 → target with easing when the stats section enters the viewport — four formats, stagger support.
Progress Bar
Reading progress in three styles — top bar, circular ring, and side rail — driven by scrollTop / scrollHeight.
Section Wipe
The next section slides over the current via sticky z-index stacking; scale-down adds depth to the transition.
Zoom Into Image
clip-path expands from a framed card to full-bleed as you scroll — the portal effect.

02 — Entrance & Exit

Element-level animations for arriving and departing — fades, slides, reveals, flips, and text staggers.

Fade In / Fade Out
Opacity 0 → 1 enter, 1 → 0 exit. The compositor handles this without re-painting — the cheapest animation.
Slide In
Translates from an off-screen edge into final position — combine with fade for natural arrival feel.
Slide Up Reveal
Text rises from below its clip boundary — two technique variants: translateY and clip-path inset.
Scale In / Zoom In
Scales from a small value to 1.0 — springy easing makes it feel like the element is landing into place.
Clip-Path Reveal
A shape mask expands to uncover the element — content is fully rendered the whole time, just progressively visible.
Curtain Reveal
A colored bar slides over then off in two stages — the reveal is hidden then exposed for maximum drama.
Split Text Reveal
Text split into chars, words, or lines — each piece animates with a staggered delay via --index.
Letter-by-Letter Stagger
Cascade mode uses CSS transition-delay per letter; typewriter mode reveals letters sequentially with a blinking cursor.
Word-by-Word Reveal
Words animate sequentially at reading speed — 80–120ms per word matches comfortable reading pace.
Blur In
Fades in while reducing blur — mimics camera focus pulling. GPU-intensive; best on single hero elements.
Flip In
Rotates from a flat plane into view using CSS 3D perspective — perspective on the parent is the key.
Bounce In
Overshoots final position then springs back — implemented with explicit @keyframes waypoints, not easing alone.
Rotate In
Spins while entering — best on radially-symmetric shapes like icons, stars, and gears.
