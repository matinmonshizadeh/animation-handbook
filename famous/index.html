The twisting ribbon of light

LoopsMade from 3 animations

Behind the headline, a wide ribbon made of fine strands of glowing color slowly twists in depth, some strands sharp and others soft, as if filmed by a camera, under a fine grain. Before 2026 the same place held a flat band of blended colors that flowed like slow paint, the look Mesh Gradient Animation makes.

Good to know

Starts
It moves on its own, and pauses when it is off screen.
On phones
A lighter version is drawn.
Reduced motion
A still picture of the ribbon is shown.

Build it with these animations

The soft light

Aurora / Northern Lights

Blurred bands of light sway like the northern lights. Best for dark backgrounds.

The grain

Grain / Film Noise Overlay

Fine grain flickers over the page, like old film. Best for editorial sites.

On the chip

WebGL Shader Animation

Moving color patterns computed for every pixel. Best for bold backgrounds.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a slowly twisting ribbon of colored light behind [the heading at the top of your page]. Draw one wide ribbon of fine strands in three or four of your own colors that drifts and turns in depth, so some strands come into sharp focus while others blur, like a camera with a shallow focus, and lay a fine grain over it so the colors feel printed rather than flat. Keep the movement slow and the heading readable on every color it passes. Draw it on the graphics chip, show a still picture of it until it is ready, and pause it when it is off screen. On phones, draw a lighter version. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show the still picture.

The headset that comes apart as you scroll

Starts on scrollMade from 2 animations

Our version

Built from the prompt below. Scroll inside the box, or press Play.

About two thirds of the way down the page, the headset stays in the middle of the screen while you scroll: its parts rise out of it layer by layer, then it tips up to show its lenses, and scrolling back puts it together.

Good to know

Starts
Scrolling plays it, forward and back.
On phones
Touch scrolling drives it the same way.
Reduced motion
A few still pictures with their text are shown instead.

Build it with these animations

Scroll plays it

Scrub Animation

Scroll plays it forward and back, like dragging a video. Best for product tours.

It stays put

Pin Animation

One part stays put while its text changes on scroll. Best for feature lists.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a scroll-driven product reveal to [your product section]. Pin the product in the middle of the screen while the visitor scrolls through a section a few screens tall, and let the scroll play a short video or a series of pictures of it, so it turns or comes apart as they scroll down and goes back together as they scroll up. Let a short headline and a paragraph about each part scroll by as usual. Show a still picture until the video or pictures are ready, and give the product a text description for screen readers. Touch scrolling drives it the same way. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show a few still pictures with their text instead.

The liquid reveal under the pointer

Starts on hoverMade from 3 animations

A large portrait stands in the middle of the screen. Wherever the pointer moves across his head, a soft, liquid-looking blob shows a second picture through it, his racing helmet, as if the portrait were being wiped away.

Good to know

Starts
Moving the pointer over the picture.
On phones
A finger dragged across the picture does the same.
Reduced motion
A plain circle shows at the pointer, without the wobble or the lag.

Build it with these animations

Follows the pointer

Cursor Follower

A dot trails your pointer and flips the colors under it. Best for portfolios.

The liquid shape

Morphing Blob

Blobs melt together, and one drop chases your pointer. Best for hero sections.

The hidden picture

Clip-Path Reveal

A growing shape uncovers it while it stays still. Best for images and banners.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a liquid reveal to [the picture at the top of your page, with a second picture hidden under it]. Stack the two pictures exactly. Wherever the pointer goes, a soft, round blob follows a little behind it, wobbling and stretching with its speed, and the hidden picture shows through the blob, as if the top picture were being wiped away. Let the blob shrink away when the pointer stops or leaves. Draw the mask on the graphics chip or with a clip, never by resizing the pictures, so it stays smooth. On touch screens, let a finger dragged across the picture do the same. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show a plain circle at the pointer, without the wobble or the lag.

The headline that comes into focus

Starts on loadMade from 3 animations

As the page opens, the headline comes in line by line, each line fading in while it rises a little and sharpens out of a soft blur; the short line under it and a small link follow the same way, then a large flat picture of the app fades in below.

Good to know

Starts
As the page opens. The whole opening is over in about a second.
On phones
Less blur is used, because it costs more there.
Reduced motion
Everything shows at once.

Build it with these animations

Lines in turn

Split Text Reveal

Text breaks into pieces that appear one after another. Best for headlines.

Blur to sharp

Blur In

Comes into focus from a blur, like a camera. Best for featured cards and photos.

Part after part

Stagger Reveal

Items in a group appear one after another as it scrolls in. Best for card grids.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a focus-in opening to [the headline, the line under it and the main picture at the top of your page]. As the page opens, show the headline line by line, each line fading in while it rises a little and sharpens out of a soft blur; then let the line under it, and any small link or button below, follow the same way, and fade the main picture in last. Keep each step short, so the whole opening is over in about a second and nobody waits to read. Animate only fading, blur and small moves, and use less blur on phones, where it costs more. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show everything at once.

The menu that drops in, link by link

Starts on pressMade from 3 animations

On a phone, the two-line menu button folds into an X while the menu fills the screen and its links drop into place one after another. On a computer, pointing at a top item opens a panel that grows down from the bar, its links appearing in turn, while the page behind blurs.

Good to know

Starts
A tap on the menu button. On computers, pointing at a top item or reaching it with the keyboard.
On phones
A tap on the menu button opens the menu over the whole screen.
Reduced motion
The menus show and hide at once.

Build it with these animations

The button

Hamburger Menu Toggle

Three lines turn into an X as the menu opens. Best for mobile menus.

Links in turn

Stagger Reveal

Items in a group appear one after another as it scrolls in. Best for card grids.

The blurred page

Glassmorphism Animated

Frosted glass cards blur the colors behind them. Best for cards and panels.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a menu that opens in steps to [the main menu in your site's header]. On phones, a tap on the menu button folds its lines into an X while the menu opens to fill the screen, and its links drop a few pixels into place one after another. On computers, pointing at a top item, or reaching it with the keyboard, opens a panel below it the same way while the page behind blurs. Keep the wait between links tiny, so the whole menu is there in about half a second, and close it faster than it opens. Make every opener a real button that tells screen readers whether its menu is open. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show and hide the menus at once.

Link previews

Starts on hoverMade from 2 animations

On any article, rest the pointer on a link and, after a short pause, a small card with the linked article's first lines and picture fades in under the link, rising slightly into place; move away and it fades out.

Good to know

Starts
Resting the pointer on a link for a moment, or reaching it with the keyboard.
On phones
There is no hover, so a tap opens the link as usual.
Reduced motion
The card shows and hides without movement.

Build it with these animations

The pause

Tooltip Reveal

A small label fades in after a short pause. Best for icon buttons.

The fade

Fade In / Fade Out

Fades in to appear and fades out to leave. Best for pop-ups and tooltips.

A prompt to build something similar

Add link previews to [the links in your articles that point to other pages]. When the pointer rests on a link for a moment, a small card with the linked page's title, first sentences and picture fades in just below the link while rising a little into place; when the pointer leaves, it fades away. The wait keeps cards from flashing while the pointer only passes by, and a card that would fall off the screen opens above the link instead. Show the same card when keyboard focus reaches the link. On touch screens, where there is no hover, let a tap open the link as usual. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show and hide the card without movement.

The color that sweeps across the headline

Starts on scrollMade from 1 animation

Far down the page, as the large headline of the section about the chip comes into view, a band of bright color sweeps across its letters once, from one end to the other, and the words settle into their plain color, as if a light had passed over them.

Good to know

Starts
When the headline first comes into view. It plays once.
On phones
Touch scrolling brings it into view the same way.
Reduced motion
The headline shows in its normal color, with no sweep.

Build it with these animations

The color sweep

Text Gradient Animation

Colors flow through the letters while the text stays still. Best for headlines.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a one-time color sweep to [the main headline of your section]. When the headline first comes into view, let a band of bright color travel across its letters once, from the start of the line to its end, and leave the text in its normal color. Paint the band as a gradient inside the letters and move only the gradient, so the words never shift or blur. Keep the sweep short, about a second, and never repeat it while the visitor reads. Keep the text readable before, during and after the sweep. Touch scrolling brings it into view the same way. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show the headline in its normal color with no sweep.

The play button that rises

Starts on hoverMade from 2 animations

Point at an album or playlist card and the card lightens a shade while a round green play button fades in, rising a few pixels into the cover's bottom corner.

Good to know

Starts
Resting the pointer on a card, or reaching it with the keyboard.
On phones
There is no hover, so the play button is always shown.
Reduced motion
The button shows and hides without movement.

Build it with these animations

The card lightens

Hover State Animation

Six ways a card can react when you point at it. Best for buttons and cards.

The button rises

Slide In

Travels into place from one edge. Best for side panels and notifications.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a hover effect to [the album or playlist cards in your grid]. When the pointer rests on a card, its background lightens a little and a round play button rises a few pixels into place at the bottom corner of the picture while it fades in; when the pointer leaves, both go back. Use only fading and transforms, and keep it quick, so moving across the grid feels light. Show the play button when a card gets keyboard focus too, and on touch screens, where there is no hover, show it all the time. Give the button a name that says what it plays. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, show and hide the button without movement.

Gray shapes while it loads

Starts on loadMade from 1 animation

Before the videos appear, gray boxes and bars in the shapes of thumbnails, round channel pictures and titles fill the page; then the real content takes their places.

Good to know

Starts
While the content loads. It stops when the content arrives.
On phones
Fewer shapes are shown.
Reduced motion
The shapes stay still.

Build it with these animations

Gray shapes

Skeleton Loader

Gray shapes hold the place of content while it loads. Best for feeds and cards.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a loading state to [the grid or feed whose content loads]. While the content loads, show gray shapes in the sizes of what is coming, such as a picture box, a round avatar and two lines of text, and let them pulse gently between lighter and darker. When the content arrives, put it exactly where the shapes were and fade it in, so nothing on the page jumps. Show only as many shapes as fit the screen, fewer on phones, and stop the pulse as soon as the content is in. Tell screen readers that the content is loading. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, keep the shapes still.

The chart that changes as you read

Starts on scrollMade from 2 animations

A chart of dots stays on screen while short paragraphs scroll past. As you scroll, the years go by and each year's dots pile up into the chart, the ones the story is about in bright color, and at some paragraphs the dots slide into a new chart; scrolling back undoes each step.

Good to know

Starts
Each time a new block of text reaches the middle of the screen.
On phones
The chart stays at the top and the text scrolls over it on cards.
Reduced motion
The steps switch without animating.

Build it with these animations

The steps

Scrollytelling

A picture beside the story changes as you read down. Best for data stories.

The chart stays

Sticky Section

A section holds still while its content changes. Best for feature tours.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a scrollytelling layout to [your story or data article]. Keep a chart or picture fixed on the screen while short blocks of text scroll past it, and change the chart each time a new block reaches the middle of the screen: light up the dots or bars the text talks about and fade the rest, so the words and the chart tell the story together. Change it smoothly, and let scrolling back undo each step. On phones, keep the chart at the top and let the text scroll over it on cards. Keep every step readable without the chart for screen readers. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, switch between the steps without animating.

The top bar that hides while you read

Starts on scrollMade from 1 animation

On a phone, scroll down into an article and the top bar moves up out of the way with the text; scroll up a little and it comes back down.

Good to know

Starts
Scrolling down hides the bar; scrolling up, even a little, brings it back.
On phones
Touch scrolling works the same way.
Reduced motion
The bar fades out and in instead of sliding.

Build it with these animations

The whole effect

Hide-on-Scroll Header

Scrolling down hides the top bar; scrolling up shows it. Best for mobile pages.

A prompt to build something similar

Add a hide-on-scroll top bar to [the header of your article pages]. When the reader scrolls down into the text, slide the bar up out of view, so the words get the whole screen; the moment they scroll up, even a little, slide it back. Always show it near the top of the page. Tell the direction by comparing each scroll position with the last one, ignore tiny moves so the bar never flickers, and move it only with a transform. Touch scrolling works the same way, and keyboard focus moving into the bar brings it back. If the visitor has reduced motion turned on, fade the bar out and in instead of sliding it.
