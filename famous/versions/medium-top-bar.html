Five small habits for a slower morning

Written by Sam Ellery5 min read

Start with a glass of water

Most mornings start in a rush. The alarm goes off, the phone lights up, and the day is already asking for answers before the kettle has boiled. A glass of water changes the pace. It tells the body the day has begun, and it gives your hands something to do while your head catches up.

Open a window

Fresh air and a little daylight wake you more gently than a bright screen does. If the weather is bad, stand near the glass for a minute and watch the street come to life.

Leave the phone alone

Keep it in another room, or at least face down. Most of what waits there can wait another half hour. When the first thing you read is your own thoughts, the morning feels longer and calmer.

If the phone is your alarm, a cheap clock will do the same job and remove the best excuse for picking it up.

Make one small thing

Boil an egg, brew tea or fold the laundry. A small task with a clear end gives an early feeling of progress, and that feeling carries into harder work later. The goal is not to be productive. It is to begin without a fight.

Go outside for ten minutes

A short walk around the block does more than most people expect. At an easy pace, ten minutes loosens the shoulders and clears the head. Stretching in the kitchen works too, on days when the weather wins.

Keep what helps

None of this has to be done perfectly, or every day. Some mornings will still be rushed. After a week, look back and keep the habits that helped. A good morning is not a strict routine. It is a few calm minutes that belong to you, set aside before everyone else asks for your time.
